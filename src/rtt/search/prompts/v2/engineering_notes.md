These rules of thumb follow from the physics of the task. They are guidance only: the validator does not enforce them, and the simulator decides.

- Friction rule. A drafted wall pushes a part toward the centre only when the wall is steep enough to beat friction: tan(90 deg - wall_draft_deg) > mu. With mu = $mu and a $margin_deg deg margin, keep wall_draft_deg at or below about $max_draft_deg deg; with a larger draft the part sticks where it lands.
- Capture rule. A pad pulls a part back to centre from a lateral error of up to about clearance_mm + pad_depth_mm x tan(wall_draft_deg), plus tip_size_mm for a chamfer. The training placements are up to $max_error_mm mm off centre across the jaw, plus up to $jitter_mm mm of jitter, so a design that should centre every placement needs a capture range of at least $error_band_mm mm. Straight walls (wall_draft_deg 0) capture only the clearance.
- Stroke cap. Each jaw slides $min_seat_mm-$max_seat_mm mm before it seats on the part, so the innermost point of a jaw (the end of a pocket wall or a V flank, or a hook lip) must stay at least $inner_mm mm from the part's centre plane. Where a part is w mm across the closing axis at the pad's height, a pocket can therefore reach at most about w/2 - $inner_mm mm past the first-contact plane (pad_depth_mm, plus tip_size_mm for a hook); a V touches on its flanks and reaches less far. At each part's narrowest height that is: $stroke_caps. A design that reaches further on any training part is rejected. Fully open, the jaws are $opening_mm mm apart.
- Print rules. Walls of at least $min_wall_mm mm, features of at least $min_feature_mm mm, at most $max_mass_g g of PLA per jaw on the heaviest part, at most $max_pieces convex pieces per jaw, and body slopes of at most $max_slope_deg deg so the jaw prints without supports. The body above the pad is generated automatically; the pad, the tip and the jaw width are yours.
